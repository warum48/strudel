# Sources

Checked 2026-11-16. Links are external; if one dies, edit freely.
Below is exactly what was taken and where the line between fact and
adaptation sits.

## 1. Overview and ensembles

- **Wikipedia — Music of Cambodia.**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Cambodia
  Taken: the three parts of classical music (pin peat, phleng kar,
  mahori), pinpeat instrumentation, instrument roles, court/temple
  context, boxing music, the loss of the tradition in the 1970s and
  its revival.
- **Wikipedia — Pinpeat.**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinpeat
  Taken: small and large line-ups, instrument list, `pin` as a lost
  and revived instrument, `Sathukar` as an opening piece, the `bot`
  repertoire for the Reamker, the parallel with Thai `piphat`.
- **Sam-Ang Sam — Cambodian music history (UMBC).**
  https://www.umbc.edu/eol/cambodia/histcmus.htm
  Taken: village/court/temple music, `kar`, `arakk`, `ayai`, `yike`,
  `basakk`; `korng skor`; the historical line from Funan to Angkor;
  the roles of `pinn`, `korng vung`, `chhing`, `sralai`.
- **Sam-Ang Sam — Pin Peat court ensemble (UMBC).**
  https://www.umbc.edu/eol/cambodia/pinpeat.htm
  Taken: the small ensemble line-up and instrument order.

## 2. Instruments

- **Wikipedia — Roneat.**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roneat
  Taken: roneat ek/thung/dek/thong, 21 bars, bamboo/wood/metal, roles
  in pinpeat and mahori.
- **Wikipedia — Samphor.**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samphor
  Taken: dimensions, two heads, up to 8 pitches, four strokes
  (`ting`, `tip`, `theung`, `tup`), leading role and tempo.
- **Wikipedia — Skor thom.**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skor_thom
  Taken: pair of big drums, sticks, tight/loose, position at the front
  of the ensemble, use in boxing music.
- **Wikipedia — Sralai.**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sralai
  Taken: quadruple reed of palm leaf, slightly conical bore, the only
  wind instrument in pinpeat, ensemble tuning to the sralai, circular
  breathing.
- **Wikipedia — Ching (instrument).**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ching_(instrument)
  Taken: size, bronze, cord, cyclical pattern, closed accent + open
  "ching", timekeeper role.
- **UNESCO / Ministry of Culture — Traditional Musical Instruments of
  Cambodia (Khean Yun, Keo Dorivan, Y Lina, Mao Lenna).**
  https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000135257
  Reference for instruments and dimensions; used as a control source,
  not as a transcription.

## 3. Tuning and scale

- **Sam-Ang Sam — Cambodian music scales (UMBC).**
  https://www2.umbc.edu/eol/cambodia/scale.htm
  Taken: two scales — 5 and 7 notes; 7 available; the Western scale
  has 7 basic + 5 auxiliary notes; comparison via a virtual xylophone.
- **Terry E. Miller, Sean Williams — The Garland Handbook of
  Southeast Asian Music.**
  Used as a general reference for seven-tone tuning, pinpeat and
  regional links. Taken: 7 tones per octave, no absolute pitch,
  heterophony.
- **Alexander J. Ellis (1885) — the 7-TET theory.**
  Classic formula: the octave is divided into seven equal steps of
  ≈ 171.4 cents. Taken as a starting point, not a dogma.
- **Garzoli, "The Myth of Equidistance in Thai Tuning" (AAWM, 2015).**
  https://iftawm.org/journal/oldsite/articles/2015b/Garzoli_AAWM_Vol_4_2.pdf
  Taken: a critique of rigid 7-TET; measured ensembles give different
  intervals and tuning drifts. That is why the docs and the track treat
  equal steps as an approximation, not a fact.

## 5. What is in the track and what is not

### Taken

- Instruments and roles: ek/thung/kong/sralai/sampho/skor/chhing.
- The seven-tone scale and the five-tone skeleton.
- Cycle, colotomy, heterophony.
- `Sathukar` as a title/homage to the opening piece.
- The boxing trio (sralai + sampho + chhing) as the idea behind BOXING.
- The absence of Western functional harmony.

### Not taken / adapted

- **4/4 to a click** — a modern grid; the tradition breathes with
  tempo.
- **Synths instead of bronze and bamboo** — a timbral adaptation.
- **Sub ≤80 Hz** — a modern low end that pinpeat does not have.
- **The C–Gb axis** — our "dark" rounding of the scale, not a
  universal Khmer harmony.
- **The title `Sathukar`** — a homage, not a transcription. The melody
  is original.

## 6. What the sources do not contain

- We did not use a notation transcription of a specific `Sathukar`.
- We did not analyse field recordings.
- We avoid claiming "the Khmer scale = exactly 7-TET": that is a
  disputed theory, not an established fact.
- We do not use `kar`/`pram` as a "two-mode" system: the sources do
  not support that as a modal system; `phleng kar` is wedding music,
  not a "kar mode".
