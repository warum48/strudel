# Pinpeat instruments

Pinpeat (ពិណពាទ្យ, pĭnpiat) is the largest traditional ensemble in
Cambodia. It performs ceremonial music for the royal court and
temples: it accompanies the royal ballet, masked theatre (lkhon khol),
shadow theatre (sbek thom) and religious ceremonies. The name comes
from `pin` (a harp, now obsolete) and `peat`/`vadya` (an ensemble of
instruments).

## 1. Two line-ups

**Small pinpeat** (pinpeat vong touch) — the core:

| Instrument | Role |
|---|---|
| roneat ek | lead high xylophone |
| kong vong thom | large gong circle |
| skor thom (2) | pair of big drums |
| sampho | double-headed drum, leads the ensemble |
| sralai | quadruple-reed oboe |

**Large pinpeat** (pinpeat vong thum) adds roneat thung, roneat dek,
kong vong toch and chhing. In both line-ups `pin` is historical and
lost; it was revived only in 2013 (Patrick Kersalé and Sonankavei).

## 2. Instruments one by one

### Roneat ek — lead xylophone

- 21 bamboo or hardwood bars, suspended on cords in a boat-shaped
  frame.
- High register; carries and ornaments the melody.
- Analogous to the Thai `ranat ek`, Burmese `pattala`.
- In the track: `triangle` + partials + a short `penv` on attack — a
  wooden "tok", not a sine beep.

### Roneat thung — lower xylophone

- Same type, lower; sits to the left of the ek.
- Plays the skeleton of the melody, not a copy: heterophony in action.
- In the track: `sine` + partials, an octave down, sparser.

### Roneat dek / roneat thong — metallophone

- 21 blackened iron bars; metallic rather than bamboo timbre. Not a
  separate layer in the track; kong vong partly covers its role.

### Kong vong thom / kong vong toch — gong circles

- Sets of small bronze gongs in a circular frame; large and small
  line-ups.
- Hold the ostinato support and the "skeleton" melody; long tail.
- In the track: `sine` + FM with an inharmonic ratio + long `decay`;
  a filter removes the "bell" quality.

### Sralai — quadruple-reed oboe

- The only wind instrument in pinpeat. The reed is made of palm leaf;
  the bore is slightly conical.
- **The ensemble tunes to the sralai**, not the other way round. The
  player uses circular breathing, so the line never stops.
- There are `sralai thom` (large) and `sralai toch` (small).
- In the track: `sawtooth` + `unison(2)` + resonant filter + a short
  `shape`. No smooth vibrato and no "jazz" phrasing: it is a reed,
  not a saxophone.

### Sampho — lead drum

- Small double-headed barrel drum, about 0.35 m wide and 0.5 m long;
  played with both hands.
- One head is larger than the other; up to eight different pitches.
- Four main strokes: `ting` (open small), `tip` (closed small),
  `theung` (open large), `tup` (closed large).
- **Sampho leads pinpeat, sets tempo and beat.** In boxing music it
  plays with sralai and chhing.
- In the track: three layers — low (theung), high (ting), ghost
  (tip/tup); each with its own pitch drop and filter.

### Skor thom — pair of big drums

- Two double-headed barrel drums, ox/cow/buffalo skin, played with
  wooden sticks.
- One is tuned tight and sounds louder/higher; the other is loose and
  duller/lower. They stand at the front of the ensemble.
- In the track: two layers, low and high, with different patterns and
  a shared "floor". No drum kit: just body and pitch drop.

### Chhing — the ensemble clock

- Small bronze finger cymbals, about 5 cm, joined by a cord.
- Play a cyclical pattern: an accented closed stroke and an
  unaccented open "ching" (onomatopoeia).
- **They regulate time and melody**, not just decorate.
- In the track: two FM layers, closed and open; closed on quarters,
  open on 2 and 4 (in BOXING on the "and").

### Krap — wooden clappers

- A pair of wooden plates; almost out of use in modern pinpeat but
  part of the historical line-up.
- In the track: a rare dry accent in BOXING and percussion-only.

### Khloy and others

- `khloy` — bamboo flute; historically could replace the sralai.
- Mahori (light court ensemble) also uses `krapeu`, `tro`,
  `skor romonea`; arak healing music uses flute, drum, `tro`,
  `chapei`, `kse diev`.
- Those instruments are not used here: the focus is pinpeat and
  percussion.

## 3. From instrument to track

| Instrument | Synthesis | What matters |
|---|---|---|
| roneat ek | triangle + partials + penv | short wooden hit, high register |
| roneat thung | sine + partials | skeleton, octave down, sparser |
| kong vong | sine + FM (`fmh` 1.41) | inharmonic bronze overtone |
| kong thom | sine + FM (`fmh` 2.76) | big gong, long tail |
| sralai | sawtooth + unison + 24db + shape | reed, not saxophone |
| sampho | sine + penv + lpf | four strokes, two heads |
| skor thom | sine/triangle + penv | tight/loose pair, sticks |
| chhing | sine + FM, short decay | bronze, "ching", cycle |
| krap | white + clip + hpf | wood, dry |

## 4. What is not here

- No drum kit: no snare on 2 and 4, no ride, no rock kick.
- No chord pad and no Western harmony.
- No "exotic" pentatonic for its own sake: 5 tones are the skeleton,
  7 tones are the melody, and both come from the Khmer scale.
- No saxophone imitation: sralai is a quadruple reed.
