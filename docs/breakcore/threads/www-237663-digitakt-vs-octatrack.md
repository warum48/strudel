### #16 · notehorn · 2025-09-03

IMO your worries aren’t minor ones.

I loved my OT MKII, it’s a unique and wonderful machine - but the two things I often grappled with:

Managing the RAM

Only having 8 tracks

Both of these things are totally manageable, and with OT ninjitsu you can probably learn to bypass the issues entirely - but I found them a PITA.

I would always have 4 tracks being used by drums and percussion and id have the 8th track as the master, so I only ever had 3 tracks to actually use for sampling. Sometimes this was fine but it felt very limiting.

My only gripes with the DT were lack of stereo, slicing and the 8 track limitations. None of those issues exist with the DTII so I consider it a bit of a powerhouse.

It can’t do everything the OT can do though so it really depends on what exactly you want.

Personally, I’m currently using an SP-404 MKII. I will likely own a DTII one day but I’m in no rush.

### #17 · chiasticon · 2025-09-03

they both have their own vibe and approach. some things are more awesome in the DT (simplicity of sampling, master track, ctrl-all, more portable) and others are more awesome in the OT (time stretch, slicing, scenes, parts, cross-fader, sample management, effects-per-track, number of LFO’s, midi sequencing, midi LFO’s, swing, slide, double the audio I/O).

it’s pretty easy to find the OT for a reasonable price. so just do that and if you don’t like it sell it off. if you prefer it over the DT, sell that. DT1’s don’t sell for a lot these days anyway, so you’re going to have to come up with the majority of the price of an OT out-of-pocket regardless; shouldn’t be too much more of a stretch to have to cover the cost of the DT too until you decide which one stays/goes (of course I don’t know your personal finances though!).

Because of limited space and notsogreat effects for master purposes, I tend to only use T8 master to bus tracks together to resample to one track. Typically for example I’d start a project by recording 2-3 tracks of hats/percussion together, maybe the second half of the sample has an additional layer (like ride cymbal for techno use). Maybe I use some scenes and jam out variations as I record. Save the sample, go to fresh “Part” and now I’ve got more free tracks again.

Being forced into commiting layers to live-bounced audio and then dealing with entire layers as a chopable “break” is dangerous but I find that kind of peril part of the fun of OT.

Also actual limiters, and nicer saturation like amp sims etc imo make it more useful to just use the DAW input as the master. You can even use a MIDI track to automate it, although you of course lose master effects on the fader, which is a bummer. But lets face it, DJ style master filter sweeps are corny anyway [image]

### #30 · personnealienee · 2025-09-19

I wouldn’t think of it as an upgrade, but more like a step to the side. Octatrack is much more suited for processing loops and stems (so, long-form audio, not one-shots), and because of its concept of record buffers allows a lot of experimental workflows. But as a straightforward sampler (for drums for example) it is quite outdated compared to more modern boxes. It is also useful for live performances because can work as a looper and simple 2-stereo channel mixer. As a midi sequencer it is comparable to Digitakt, plus it has arpeggiator
