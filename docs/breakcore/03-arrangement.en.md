# 03 · Form and arrangement (EN)

Breakcore is a drum solo with dramaturgy. Everything else is scenery.

---

## 1. Form as a "solo"

- Definition: "electronically composed drum solos with the drum solos being the main
  element of a track with other sounds being a backdrop" `[6643#1]`.
- Abstraction is allowed: "breakcore is a rather abstract genre that doesn't
  NECESSARILY require melody, rythm or soundquality" `[18985#67]`.
- But the genre rests on the **BREAKBEAT**: "breakcore's specifically about the
  BREAKBEAT" `[173927#51]`.

**Practical conclusion:** melody, bass and pads are the backdrop that frames the
drums. If the track is still interesting with the drums removed, it is not breakcore.

---

## 2. Drums = rhythm guitar + lead guitar

The best structural metaphor in the corpus:

> "Structure the drums like both a lead guitar and rhythm guitar in a rock song …
> have a drum element keep the rhythm like a kick snare accent, or a hat pattern …
> and have the other drum elements act as a lead" `[6643#47]`.

Layout:
- **Rhythm layer:** kick + snare + hat, stable, almost never changes.
- **Lead layer:** chopping, rolls, glitch, snare rushes, break switches.
- **Solo:** a 4–8 bar passage where the lead layer is the only drum element.

This solves the genre's main problem: "dense all the time" → no dramaturgy.

---

## 3. Mutation cadence: 4 bars

- "every 4 bars/pattern … start with a new rhythmic/melodic idea with the drums and
  generally mutate and change it over the next set of bars. Then start a new idea"
  `[44354#6]`.
- Before composing, chart the **intensity curve** of a reference: where it plays
  "simple and airy" and where destruction begins `[44354#6]`.
- BotB: "Every drumpattern is different, I make them from scratch", but a familiar
  groove returns later `[18985#46]`.
- Clone patterns and add small variations: "one pattern has the snare on the 3 and 5
  beat out of 8 … another has the snare roll on the 4" `[6643#30]`.

**Anti-pattern:** 18 patterns built from 3 variations `[6643#15]`.

---

## 4. The subtractive method

- Start from the **most complex** version of the pattern and remove `[173927]`.
- Reduce to "only what is necessary": 1-bar patterns, 3 tracks `[173927]`.
- Simple → complex → simple again: contrast makes complexity audible `[6643#7, #48]`.
- Leave "sonic breathing room": without pauses a wall of noise stops reading `[6643#7]`.

---

## 5. Tempo and meter within the form

- Genre range: 150–360 BPM `[26084#3]`; 5/8 as a structural device `[78570#1]`.
- Meter changes **inside** a track (not straight 4/4 all the way) `[6643#23]`.
- At high tempo, write the other elements in half-time `[6643#47]`.
- Slow breakcore exists: 90 BPM with clock/timing manipulation `[5102#18]`.

**Do not confuse:** tempo does not make the genre. 4/4 is acceptable if the break
leads `[6643#49]`.

---

## 6. A typical track map (working, not dogma)

| Section | Bars | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | 8–16 | one break + sub, minimal layers, 1/8 anchor |
| Establishment | 16 | rhythm layer stable, lead layer introduces 1–2 techniques |
| Development | 16–32 | mutations every 4 bars, snare rush / break switch added |
| "Solo" | 4–8 | lead layer alone, rhythm layer off or half-off |
| Return | 16 | familiar groove + a new element that becomes the next lead |
| Exit | 8–16 | peel layers off, leave kick/break for mixing |

Check against real references: "transcribe arrangements, not just drums" `[173927]`.

---

## 7. Tension and contrast

- Tension: "Sequence the melody or bass nicely in time, and get totally spastic with
  the breaks in the background" `[18985#66]`.
- Call-and-return between drums and bass `[173927]`.
- "Horizontal" vs "vertical" IDM: either ideas flow into each other (closer to a solo),
  or establishment → build → break. Breakcore is usually the latter, but the former
  gives "narrative".
- Density change matters more than note change: dropping a layer for 2 bars is an
  event.
- Silence/pause is an element, not a mistake `[173927]`.

---

## 8. What makes a track "cheap"

- Constant maximum density with no contrast `[6643#7]`.
- No fills and no snare rushes `[6643#14]`.
- Locked 4/4 for the whole track `[6643#23]`.
- Melody/bass that does not match the drum groove `[8682#1]`.
- An atonal backdrop with no tonal balance `[6643#48]`.

---

## 9. Arrangement checklist

- [ ] A rhythm layer and a lead layer exist and are audibly distinct.
- [ ] There is at least one "drum solo" passage.
- [ ] There is a lower-density passage before the return.
- [ ] Mutations at least every 4–8 bars.
- [ ] The familiar groove returns at least once.
- [ ] Entry and exit allow DJ mixing (kick/break without melody).
- [ ] The meter is either deliberately stable or deliberately changing.
