# Big Beat — best examples (annotated listening)

Sources for the picks: NPR/KEXP *The Big Beat Revolution: 11 Essential Songs*
(originators pick the tracks), Mixmag *10 best big beat tracks released pre-'98*,
RTÉ *Top 100 90s Dance Anthems*, AllMusic *Big Beat Song Highlights*, Sonaa
*Big Beat: history, key tracks and production guide*, plus Wikipedia's chart
facts. Links: `05-sources.en.md`.

## 1. Core canon — what to listen to first

| Track | Artist / year | Why it matters (from the sources) |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical Beats | The Dust Brothers (Chemical Brothers), 1994 | Liam Howlett: "that record singlehandedly created the sound" — slower dance record, breakbeat + acid riff + hip-hop influence, just builds and drops. |
| Block Rockin' Beats | The Chemical Brothers, 1997 | Schoolly D "Gucci Again" sample; intricate percussion, dense plunging bass drum, acid synths and a screeching climax (Mixmag). RTÉ #32. |
| Brothers Gonna Work It Out | The Chemical Brothers, 1998 | Fatboy Slim's "blueprint": breakbeats, acid-house builds, a pop hook — "cherry-picked the best from rave and hip-hop". |
| Private Psychedelic Reel | The Chemical Brothers, 1997 | Jon Carter: big beat's peak — layered so densely the Beatles' publishers thought it was a sample; soul + darkness in an instrumental. |
| The Rockafeller Skank | Fatboy Slim, 1998 | Stuttered pitched vocal phrase, surf-guitar riff, song structure (Sonaa). RTÉ #42; #76 US. |
| Praise You | Fatboy Slim, 1998 | Camille Yarbrough sample; the commercial peak, #36 US, RTÉ #9. |
| Right Here, Right Now | Fatboy Slim, 1998 | Built on the intro of James Gang's *Ashes, The Rain And I* — the canonical example of "unexpected source" sampling (MusicRadar). RTÉ list. |
| Firestarter | The Prodigy, 1996 | Breakbeat turned punk: guitar riff, shouted vocal, stadium energy (Sonaa). RTÉ #6, #30 US. |
| Breathe | The Prodigy, 1997 | Wu-Tang "Da Mystery of Chessboxin'" whiplash sample, guitar riff, pacy bass, punk vocal; UK #1 (Mixmag). |
| Spybreak! | Propellerheads, 1997 | Organic hi-hat + sneaky spy bassline; the *Matrix* moment; "more elegant" big beat (Sonaa). |
| Take California | Propellerheads, 1998 | Wall of Sound: huge energy, dancefloor "fists in the air"; first iPod commercial (NPR, Classic Pop). |
| Busy Child | The Crystal Method, 1997 | Eric B. & Rakim "Juice (The Ledge)" sample, acid-soaked, big drops, orchestral crescendo (Mixmag). |
| Ooh La La | Wiseguys, 1998 | Hooky commercial sample; with "Rockafeller Skank" the biggest tunes of the genre (Aston Harvey, NPR). |
| Everybody Needs A 303 | Fatboy Slim, 1996/97 | Warped guitar riff, uplifting vocal sample, thick 303 lines at the end (Mixmag). |
| Hey Boy Hey Girl | The Chemical Brothers, 1999 | Late-peak crossover anthem, RTÉ #4. |
| Da Funk | Daft Punk, 1995 | Damian Harris: mid-tempo bridge from hip-hop/funk to house tempo — "before big beat was defined". |

## 2. Scene picks (NPR's 11, by the people who were there)

- Chemical Beats — Dust Brothers (1994), picked by Liam Howlett (Prodigy).
- Give the Po' Man a Break — Fatboy Slim (1996), Martin Whiteman: "all the
  elements... it's all about the breakbeat" pushed through a compressor.
- Da Funk — Daft Punk (1995), Damian Harris (Skint).
- Brothers Gonna Work It Out — Chemical Brothers (1998), Norman Cook.
- Trickshot — Ceasefire (1995), Will White (Propellerheads): film sample +
  funk break + big guitars.
- Ooh La La — Wiseguys (1998), Aston Harvey (Freestylers).
- Song to the Siren — Dust Brothers (1992), Barry Ashworth (Dub Pistols).
- Take California — Propellerheads (1998), Alvin Collins (Wall of Sound).
- Private Psychedelic Reel — Chemical Brothers (1997), Jon Carter.
- My Mate Paul — David Holmes (1997), Alex Gifford: tight beat, huge rolling
  reverb, "cinematic".
- Do You Wanna Hear It — The Nubian Crackers (1993), Mekon: New York
  instrumental hip-hop, sped-up breaks — one of the genre's roots.

Also named by Mixmag: Hardknox "Psychopath" (siren, beatbox, pneumatic drum),
Groove Armada "Vanilla", Lo Fidelity Allstars "Kool Rok Bass", Cut La Roc.
AllMusic highlights add "Setting Sun", "Life Is Sweet", "Star 69", "Come With
Us", FSOL "We Have Explosive".

## 3. Albums and scene infrastructure

- *Exit Planet Dust* (1995), *Dig Your Own Hole* (1997), *Surrender* (1999) —
  Chemical Brothers; the middle one is Wikipedia's "critical and commercial
  peak" anchor and RIAA gold.
- *The Fat of the Land* (1997) — Prodigy; #1 Billboard 200, 2.6M US copies.
- *You've Come a Long Way, Baby* (1998) — Fatboy Slim; platinum in 1999.
- *Decksandrumsandrockandroll* (1998) — Propellerheads; Mercury-nominated,
  *Spybreak!* and *Take California* (Classic Pop).
- Labels/clubs: Skint + Big Beat Boutique (Brighton), Wall of Sound + Heavenly
  Sunday Social (London), City of Angels (US). Wipeout soundtracks and
  *The Matrix* carried the sound into games/film (Wikipedia).

## 4. Listening checklist (what to copy musically)

1. Is the **break the hook**? Can you hum its rhythm without the bass?
2. Where is the **compressor** audible (pumping, glued transients)?
3. Is there exactly **one** acid/303-like line, with automation?
4. Does the "vocal" moment arrive as a short **event**, not a sung chorus?
5. Count **build → silence → drop**: how many bars, and how long is the stop?
6. Is the riff a 60s/70s-style sample or a short synth stab in the same spot?
7. Does the low end stay mono and dry while the top gets tape/delay/reverb?

## 5. What we cannot copy literally

Original big beat is built on lifted funk/rock/vocal samples. This repo
produces **instrumental synth tracks** for strudel.cc: no vocals, no acoustic
imitation, no uncleared samples (`docs/rules.en.md`, `docs/pitfalls.en.md`).
Copy the *functions* — compressed break, acid line, riff-like stab, punctuating
event, build/drop — using `github:yaxu/clean-breaks`, `Dirt-Samples` and
synthesis. Where a famous track uses a shouted vocal, use a filtered stab or a
resonant noise sweep.
